01 / 07

Screening tenure reviewers is a manual integrity check with career-level stakes

Every tenure case depends on letters from reviewers at arm's length from the candidate. Checking that independence is manual, inconsistent across departments, and invisible until something goes wrong.

04 / 07

Value is mostly risk avoided, not hours saved

One restarted or challenged tenure case outweighs a year of manual checking, for the candidate, the department and the provost's office.

Time is the secondary benefit. Across a large customer base the base case is roughly 39k–59k staff hours a year savedAssumption, after allowing for the chair confirming each reviewer's identity and checking Insufficient data names by hand. The saving depends mostly on how often data is insufficient, not on how fast chairs click.

06 / 07

Defensibility is the workflow and trust, not data alone

Competitors with their own research data could copy a data check. What's hard to copy is a check inside the workflow where the reviewer list is built and the decision is recorded, with evidence a faculty committee trusts. So the plan competes on integration and trust.
